\begin{abstract}
This work explores an end-to-end workflow for generating, checking, and exporting formally verified quantum circuits.
The central idea is to place a Lean 4 proof assistant in the feedback loop of a language-model system: a model proposes circuit definitions and correctness proofs, Lean checks them against a small quantum circuit library, and elaborated failures guide subsequent repair attempts.
This extended abstract sketches the project structure, the verification pipeline, and the role of reusable Lean infrastructure for gates, tensors, circuits, goals, and solutions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation}

Quantum circuit descriptions are compact, but correctness arguments are often algebraically dense and easy to get subtly wrong.
Our workflow treats formal verification as a practical part of circuit generation rather than a separate after-the-fact audit, building on the proof-assistant approach exemplified by Lean~\citep{moura2021lean}.

\section{Project Structure}

The repository is organized around a Lean 4 library, a collection of benchmark goals, and solution files that discharge those goals.
Core definitions, challenge statements, and completed proofs are separated into library, goal, and solution modules.
Development notes and research references are maintained as a lightweight project wiki under \texttt{notes/}.

\section{Verification Workflow}

The intended workflow has four stages.
First, a user specifies a quantum algorithm or circuit.
Second, an LLM generates a Lean formalization containing a circuit definition and correctness statement.
Third, Lean checks the proposed proof and returns structured diagnostics on failure.
Finally, verified circuits can be exported to executable representations such as OpenQASM or Qiskit.

\section{Lean Infrastructure}

The Lean library currently emphasizes finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces, tensor-index bookkeeping, single-qubit gates, gate placement, controlled operations, and list-based circuit composition.
This infrastructure is designed to make small concrete circuit identities tractable while leaving room for larger algorithmic proofs such as QFT and GHZ preparation.

\section{Outlook}

The extended-abstract version of this paper should clarify the architecture and research contribution before expanding into a full evaluation.
Near-term additions include a sharper problem statement, a worked example, and a concise comparison with simulation-based verification and existing quantum formalization projects.
