\begin{abstract}
Large language models (LLMs) have shown competence in abstract mathematical reasoning.
Some frontier models have even been able to solve open problems in combinatorics.
However, these models often fail to consistently produce correct, verifiable results, and require
much human intervention and iteration.
Furthermore, LLMs struggle in the niche domain of quantum computing, where the potential training material is
limited compared to a more general and well-studied field such as combinatorics.
We propose an agentic framework for abstract quantum computing algorithm design,
where core definitions and axioms of the field are defined in Lean, goals can be formally specified,
and correctness is checked at every step with the Lean proof-checker.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Quantum computing has the potential to exponentially speed up
many classical computing algorithms \cite{brandao2010exponential}.
However, solutions to quantum computing problems are often hard to verify,
given that quantum programs cannot be efficiently simulated on a classical computer,
and access to currently-available quantum machines is very expensive.
As a result, many programs can only be verified analytically.
This presents as particular challenge for LLM agents.

So far, most attempts to use LLMs for quantum circuit synthesis have
used Qiskit or OpenQASM as the underlying environment; circuits are
written as code in these languages, then executed on a quantum simulator,
and the results are compared with the expected answer.
We propose a different approach: quantum computing foundamentals can be formalized in a
theorem assistant language such as Lean, then goals such as circuit synthesis
can be posed formally. The LLM is then instructed to not only provide code to construct
a circuit, but also a proof-of-correctness, allowing for automatic verification
without the need for expensive numerical simulation.
